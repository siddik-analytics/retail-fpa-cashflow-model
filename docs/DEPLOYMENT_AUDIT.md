### Verdict: **NOT READY**

This verdict is about publishability today, not build quality. The underlying model is genuinely strong — materially stronger than most Excel work presented as an FP&A portfolio piece. The cash engine reconciles perfectly, the scenario engine is a true rebuild rather than a percentage overlay, and the management commentary is live-linked rather than typed.

It is not publishable as-is because of one material calculation error in the exit scenario (P0-1) that flows into published management conclusions and is not caught by any of the thirty controls, plus a small set of commentary statements that the model's own numbers contradict. These are precise, bounded fixes — the artifact is roughly six targeted corrections away from READY, not a rebuild.

**No privacy or confidentiality blocker exists.** The file is safe to publish from a data-protection standpoint.

## Critical Findings

Severity: **P0** deployment blocker · **P1** material credibility issue · **P2** important polish/robustness · **P3** optional enhancement

### P0

**P0-1 — Exit scenario never pays August store payroll**
*Sheet:* Scenarios · *Range:* `C165:O165` (Scenario 3 block, "Payroll - store and corporate")

The formula is `=(Assumptions!$B$46*(1+$E$8)*C152+Assumptions!$B$91)*'13W Cash Flow'!C$69` — store payroll is multiplied by the store-open factor for the week of *payment*, not the week of *accrual*. The store trades Weeks 1–4 (3–30 August) and closes at the end of Week 4. August payroll is paid at month end, which falls in Week 5, when the open factor is already 0. The result is that a month of genuinely earned store wages, **S$18,500**, is never paid in the forecast.

*Why it matters:* It understates Scenario 3 outflows by S$18,500 and overstates every published exit-case cash figure. The effect is not cosmetic:

| Scenario 3 measure | As built | Corrected | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Week 13 closing cash | 128,483 | 109,983 | −18,500 |
| Lowest projected cash | 97,330 | 78,830 | −18,500 |
| Headroom at lowest point | (22,670) | (41,170) | −18,500 |
| Weeks below minimum buffer | 4 | 6 | +2 |

Those figures are quoted verbatim on the Dashboard (`A18`) and in Management Insight 7. No control detects it: controls 19–21 test only that Scenario 1 reproduces the base case, and there is no roll-forward or completeness control on the Scenario 2 and 3 blocks. The monthly run-rate P&L is *correct* — it is a steady-state view and rightly excludes a one-off transitional cost — so the error is confined to the 13-week cash path.

*Remediation:* Separate accrual from payment. Drive store payroll off the number of weeks the store actually traded in the payment month, e.g. multiply by the count of open weeks in that month divided by the weeks in that month, or add an explicit "final payroll settlement" line to the exit-cost schedule. Then add a control asserting that total store payroll paid across the 13 weeks equals store payroll accrued while the store was open.

### P1

**P1-1 — Document metadata identifies the generator, not the author**
*Scope:* `docProps/core.xml`, `docProps/app.xml`

`dc:creator` is `openpyxl`. `Application` is `LibreOffice/24.2.7.2$Linux_X86_64`. `fileVersion appName` is `Calc`. Title, subject and company are empty.

*Why it matters:* The repository will present this as a demonstration of advanced Excel FP&A modelling. Any reviewer who opens File → Info sees that the workbook was generated by a Python library and last written by LibreOffice on Linux. That is not a data leak, but it invites the question "did you build this or generate it?" at exactly the wrong moment, and empty document properties read as unfinished.

*Remediation:* Set `dc:creator` to your own name and add a real title and subject. **Do not** rewrite `Application` to claim Microsoft Excel — that would be a false statement about the artifact, and the remediation phase should not manufacture provenance. If the model was script-generated, the honest and actually impressive framing is to say so in the repository README and publish the generator under `src/`; "I built a parameterised model generator" is a stronger claim than "I typed this in Excel", not a weaker one.

**P1-2 — Scenario narrative contradicts the model's own threshold**
*Sheet:* Scenarios · *Cell:* `A79`

The trade-off note reads: Scenario 3 "only beats the improvement case if online migration is far higher than the 25% assumed here." The model's own calculation at `E72` puts the required migration at **25.79%** against 25.0% assumed — a gap of 0.79 percentage points. The two scenarios differ by S$255 of monthly operating profit on S$27,000, under 1%.

*Why it matters:* "Far higher" misdescribes a result the workbook computes three rows above. Management Insight 7 states the same fact correctly ("needs 25.8% online migration merely to match the improvement case — against the 25.0% assumed"), so the workbook contradicts itself. An experienced reviewer who checks one number will check this one, and finding the narrative overstated undermines trust in the commentary generally.

*Remediation:* Rewrite `A79` to match the arithmetic — the honest reading is that the two cases are within a rounding error on profit, and the decision therefore turns on cash timing, execution risk and reversibility rather than on run-rate profit.

**P1-3 — Dashboard headline conclusions will clip**
*Sheet:* Dashboard · *Range:* `A15:O18`

The four "WHAT THIS MEANS" rows are merged across A:O (about 123.6 character-units) with `wrap_text` on, but each row is fixed at `ht="24"` with `customHeight="true"`, which disables Excel's auto-fit. At 10pt Calibri a 24pt row holds roughly 1.8 lines. Three of the four strings need two:

| Cell | Length | Lines needed |
|---|---:|---:|
| `A15` | 139 chars | ~2 |
| `A16` | 128 chars | ~1 |
| `A17` | 192 chars | ~2 |
| `A18` | 182 chars | ~2 |

*Why it matters:* These four sentences are the single most screenshot-worthy element in the workbook and the thing a GitHub visitor or Upwork client reads first. Truncated conclusions on a dashboard are the classic tell of a model that was never printed or presented.

*Caveat:* Text metrics here are calculated, not rendered — I could not open the file in Excel. Actual wrapping depends on word boundaries and exact font metrics. Treat this as a high-probability defect requiring visual confirmation, not a certainty. The `customHeight="true"` flag is the decisive part: whatever the exact wrap, Excel will not self-correct.

*Remediation:* Raise those rows to 32–40pt, or set `customHeight="false"` so Excel auto-fits, or shorten the generated sentences. Confirm visually before screenshotting.

**P1-4 — Minimum cash buffer note is not supported by the model**
*Sheet:* Assumptions · *Cell:* `D13`

The note reads "Board-set buffer: c.2.5 months of fixed cost cover." The threshold is S$120,000 (`B13`). The model's own total monthly fixed cost base is S$80,450 (`B94` = 44,450 + 6,300 + 29,700). That is **1.49 months** of cover, not 2.5. The 2.5 figure only works against store-only fixed costs (S$44,450 → 2.70 months), which is not what "fixed cost cover" means when the workbook itself defines a total fixed cost base one row away.

*Why it matters:* It is a one-line division that a CFO or interviewer will do in their head, and the model supplies both numbers.

*Remediation:* Either restate the note as approximately 1.5 months of total fixed cost cover, or make it a formula — `=B13/B94` — so it can never drift from the inputs again.

**P1-5 — Insight 3 overstates the driver ranking**
*Sheet:* Management Insights · *Cell:* `B12`

The text claims sales and gross margin move store profit "roughly three times as hard as a proportionate move in rent or payroll." The workbook's own driver sensitivity (`Store Break-Even` F65:F69) gives swings of 8,669 (sales) and 9,596 (gross margin) against 4,400 (rent) and 3,700 (payroll) — ratios of **1.97× to 2.59×**, not three.

*Why it matters:* Same class of problem as P1-2: hardcoded prose asserting something the live model contradicts. The underlying point — that this is a trading question rather than a cost-cutting question — survives at 2×, so the fix costs nothing analytically.

*Remediation:* Change to "roughly twice as hard", or better, make the multiple a live `TEXT()` reference to the sensitivity output so it cannot go stale.

### P2

**P2-1 — Payment timing flags silently drop payments for days 26–31**
*Sheet:* 13W Cash Flow · *Range:* `C64:O68`

Each flag tests `DATE(YEAR(week_end), MONTH(week_end), payment_day)` against the week window — it only ever looks at the *week-ending* month. For a week that spans a month boundary, a payment day falling in the week-*starting* month is invisible. Simulating every payment day against the current calendar:

| Payment day | Flags raised | Should be | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1, 5, 10, 15, 20 | 3 | 3 | correct |
| 26, 27 | 2 | 3 | one payment lost |
| 28, 29, 30 | 1 | 3 | two payments lost |
| 31 | 0 | 2 | all payments lost |

The configured days (1, 5, 10, 15, 20) are all safe, and I separately confirmed the flags stay correct for every weekly roll of the start date from 3 Aug to 28 Sep — so the advertised one-cell roll genuinely works today. But `Assumptions!B78:B82` are amber input cells the user is invited to edit, and setting rent to "paid on the 28th" would quietly delete S$44,000 of outflows with all thirty controls still reading PASS.

*Remediation:* Test both candidate months, e.g. `=IF(OR(AND(DATE(YEAR(C$6),MONTH(C$6),day)>=C$6,DATE(YEAR(C$6),MONTH(C$6),day)<=C$7),AND(DATE(YEAR(C$7),MONTH(C$7),day)>=C$6,DATE(YEAR(C$7),MONTH(C$7),day)<=C$7)),1,0)`, and add data validation capping payment days at 28. Extend control 9 as below.

**P2-2 — Control 9 cannot detect compensating errors**
*Sheet:* Controls · *Cell:* `D15`

`=SUM('13W Cash Flow'!C64:O64)+SUM(C69:O69)+SUM(C65:O65)` compared against an expected 9. It proves the three flag rows *total* nine triggers, not that each triggers three times. A model where rent fires four times and the loan twice passes cleanly. Given P2-1 is exactly the failure mode this control exists to catch, it is the wrong shape.

*Remediation:* Replace with `=ABS(SUM(C64:O64)-3)+ABS(SUM(C69:O69)-3)+ABS(SUM(C65:O65)-3)` expected 0, or split into three controls.

**P2-3 — Error-scan controls are partly defeated by IFERROR**
*Sheet:* Controls · *Range:* `D32:D36`

Controls 26–30 use `SUMPRODUCT(--ISERROR(range))`. The workbook contains **73 `IFERROR(...,0)` wrappers** (Channel Profitability 31, Store Break-Even 24, Scenarios 18). Every one of them converts an upstream `#REF!` or `#DIV/0!` into a plausible-looking zero *before* the scan can see it. A broken denominator would surface as a 0.0% margin on the Dashboard with MODEL STATUS still reading PASS.

Two further gaps: there is **no error scan on the Assumptions tab at all** (which holds 29 formulas including the derived cash groupings that feed every other sheet), and the Dashboard scan covers only `A4:O30`, missing the chart-data block at rows **100–129** that actually feeds all four charts.

*Remediation:* Extend the scans to Assumptions and to `Dashboard!A100:C129`. Add a small number of positive-value assertions on cells where `IFERROR` is used on a critical path — for example, that total net revenue is greater than zero and that each channel contribution margin lies between −1 and 1 — so a masked error cannot present as a clean zero.

**P2-4 — No integrity controls on Scenario 2 and 3 blocks**
*Sheet:* Controls

Controls 19–21 test only that Scenario 1 reproduces the base case. The Scenario 2 and 3 weekly blocks (rows 115–145 and 148–178) — 66 rows carrying the exit case that drives the central recommendation — have no roll-forward, continuity or subtotal control. I verified independently that all three blocks currently reconcile to zero break, but the model does not verify it for the next person. This is the structural gap that let P0-1 through.

*Remediation:* Replicate controls 1 and 2 across all three blocks.

**P2-5 — Only 3 of 8 Dashboard KPI cards have link controls**
*Sheet:* Controls · *Range:* `D29:D31`

Controls 23–25 tie opening cash, Week 13 closing cash and the break-even gap. Untested: lowest cash point (`I6`), cash runway (`M6`), 13-week net revenue (`A11`), gross margin (`E11`) and store profit (`I11`). The Dashboard footer asserts "no dashboard number is keyed independently" — the controls substantiate that claim for three of eight cards.

**P2-6 — No data validation anywhere in the workbook**
*Scope:* all sheets

Zero `dataValidation` elements. The README sheet states the amber cells "are the only cells intended to be edited" and the Assumptions tab is "the only sheet you need to edit", but nothing prevents a collection percentage above 1, a negative settlement lag, a closure week of 40, or a text entry in a rate cell. For a workbook whose Controls tab is a headline feature, absent input validation is a visible omission — controls catch errors after the fact, validation prevents them.

*Remediation:* Add validation on the percentage inputs (0–1), the day-of-month inputs (1–28, per P2-1), the week-number inputs (0–13) and the scenario levers.

**P2-7 — Consignment commission uses a different base in P&L and cash**
*Sheets:* Channel Profitability `F15`, 13W Cash Flow `C15:O15`

The P&L charges commission on **gross** sales (`=-F5*Assumptions!$B$41`); the cash forecast nets it from **net** sales (`INDEX($C$75:$O$75,...)*(1-Assumptions!$B$41)`, where row 75 is already net of discounts). The two agree today only because consignment discounts (`B25`) are set to 0. Set that input to anything non-zero and the P&L and cash views diverge with no control detecting it.

*Remediation:* Pick one basis — net is consistent with every other variable cost line — and align both.

**P2-8 — Scenarios print area truncates its own headline**
*Sheet:* Scenarios · *Defined name:* `Print_Area = $A$1:$M$79`

Row 3 is merged `A3:P3` and carries the dynamic "Preferred scenario" banner. Row 79 is merged `A79:O79` and carries the trade-off narrative. The synthetic-data badge sits in `P1` and the navigation link in `P2`. All four extend beyond column M and will be clipped on print or PDF export. Every other sheet's print area reaches its last used column; this one does not.

*Remediation:* Extend to `$A$1:$P$79`.

**P2-9 — Inventory note implies a reconciliation that does not hold**
*Sheet:* Assumptions · *Cell:* `D75`

The note on weekly replenishment (58% of weekly COGS) reads "Balance of stock intake comes from the scheduled AW26 buy below", which implies the two together fund cost of sales. They do not: replenishment of S$174,023 plus the AW26 buy of S$235,000 is S$409,023 against 13-week COGS of S$300,040 — **136% of COGS**, an inventory build of about S$109,000. The build is economically sensible ahead of an autumn/winter season, and the workbook openly states it carries no balance sheet, but the note describes a different relationship than the numbers show.

*Remediation:* Reword to state plainly that the AW26 buy is a deliberate pre-season stock build, and consider a memo line quantifying it — an FP&A reviewer will ask where S$109,000 of stock went, and answering before they ask is the stronger position.

**P2-10 — KPI card label and value alignment disagree**
*Sheet:* Dashboard · *Rows:* 5–7, 10–12

Card labels (`A5`) and captions (`A7`) are left-aligned; the value (`A6`) is centre-aligned within the same merged card. The number floats centre while its label and caption sit hard left. On a screenshot this reads as unintentional.

*Remediation:* Left-align the values, or centre all three. Left is the more common convention for finance KPI cards.

**P2-11 — "Preferred scenario" is decided on a 0.9% difference by a single metric**
*Sheet:* Scenarios · *Cell:* `A3`

The banner selects the preferred scenario as `MAX` of monthly run-rate operating profit. Scenario 2 returns S$27,343 and Scenario 3 S$27,088 — a gap of S$255, under 1%. The banner presents that as a determination, in the first line of the sheet, without a materiality qualifier, and the selection ignores the S$96,000 of exit cash, the four weeks below buffer, and the irreversibility of closure. `MATCH` also silently resolves ties to the first scenario.

*Remediation:* Either widen the selector to a documented multi-criteria rule, or reframe the banner as "highest run-rate operating profit" — a statement of fact — and let the trade-off note carry the recommendation. The Management Insights sheet already does this correctly.

**P2-12 — Missing receipts-completeness control**
*Sheet:* Controls

Nothing verifies that cash collected reconciles to sales plus opening debtors less closing debtors. I tested this independently and every channel ties exactly (store, e-commerce, events and consignment all reconcile to zero difference against the intended lag logic), so the model is right — but this is the single most valuable cash control available and it is absent. A change to any settlement percentage or lag could break collection without a single control failing.

### C. 13-Week Cash Flow Audit

**Reconciliation testing — all identities verified independently against cached values:**

| Test | Weeks tested | Breaks |
|---|---:|---:|
| Opening + receipts − outflows = closing | 13 | **0** |
| Opening cash = prior week closing | 12 | **0** |
| Total receipts = sum of receipt lines | 13 | **0** |
| Total outflows = sum of outflow lines | 13 | **0** |
| Total net sales = sum of 4 channels | 13 | **0** |
| Total COGS = sum of 4 channels | 13 | **0** |
| Row totals (col P) = sum of weeks C:O | 25 rows | **0** |

Maximum residual across all tests: 5.8×10⁻¹¹ — floating point only.

**Receipts completeness — tested against the intended lag logic:**

| Channel | Expected | Actual | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Store | 249,372.28 | 249,372.28 | 0.00 |
| E-commerce | 293,437.60 | 293,437.60 | 5.8e-11 |
| Events | 100,104.00 | 100,104.00 | 0.00 |
| Consignment | 70,775.60 | 70,775.60 | 0.00 |

Net sales of 747,630 against receipts of 713,689 leaves S$60,681 of closing receivables — correctly composed of the Week 13 store and e-commerce tails and four weeks of unremitted consignment, plus S$31,157 of consignment commission that is netted at source and correctly never appears as a cash cost. No leakage, no double counting.

**Payment timing.** Every monthly item triggers exactly three times across the 13 weeks (rent, payroll, loan, utilities, software, professional fees — verified individually). August rent falls on 1 August, before the Week 1 Monday of 3 August, and is correctly outside the window. The `EOMONTH` payroll test and the `DATE`/`AND` day-of-month tests are a genuinely good design — timing is derived from the calendar, not from hardcoded week numbers. The date-roll claim holds: I simulated shifting the start Monday by 1–8 weeks and every monthly item still fired exactly three times.

Two caveats. The flags are fragile for payment days 26–31 (P2-1). And the "roll the forecast by changing this one date" claim is true only for the calendar-driven items — the GST week (`B88` = 9), the four AW26 buy weeks (`C115:C118`) and the two opening-receivable balances (`B71`, `B72`) are all week-numbered or point-in-time and do **not** roll with the date. Rolling the model forward requires updating those too.

**Conceptual treatment of revenue vs cash.** Sound and clearly the work of someone who has built one of these for real. Channel-specific settlement (92% store / 85% e-commerce / 100% events / 4-week consignment lag) is realistic and each is explained. Consignment receipts correctly arrive net of the 30% partner commission, so the commission never double-counts as an outflow. Events are banked at the event. Opening receivables (`B71` = 1,520; `B72` = 3,240) are consistent with 8% and 15% of a prior week at the stated run-rates — they were not picked arbitrarily.

**Liquidity reporting.** Minimum threshold, weekly headroom, lowest cash point with its week and date, count of weeks below buffer, and two runway measures (to nil cash and to threshold). The runway formula correctly returns text when cash-generative rather than a negative number. ### E. Store Break-Even Audit

**The core calculation is economically and mathematically correct.**

Contribution margin of 52.4% is built as gross margin 58.0% less variable costs 5.6% (merchant fees 1.8% + casual labour 3.0% + other direct 0.8%) — correctly excluding all fixed costs. Break-even = fixed store costs / contribution margin % = 44,450 / 0.524 = **S$84,828 per month**. Current net sales of S$82,720 sit S$2,108 (2.5%) below, giving a margin of safety of −2.5% and a monthly loss of S$1,105. Every figure ties to the Channel Profitability monthly averages, and control 17 independently proves that sales at the break-even level produce a nil result (verified: 84,828.24 × 0.524 − 44,450 = 0).

Alternative routes to break-even are all correct: a 2.5% sales uplift, a 1.34pp gross-margin uplift (`=-C25/C5-C17`), or a S$1,105 (5.0%) rent reduction.

**Sensitivity behaviour** — I traced both grids formula-by-formula and both behave correctly:

- *Grid 1* (`C50:G52`), profit by sales change × gross margin: `=$C$5*(1+sales_chg)*($B50-$C$15)+$C$25`. Revenue scales, contribution margin recomputes as GM less the unchanged variable rate, fixed costs stay fixed. The base cell (0% / 58%) returns −1,104.72, tying exactly to `C27`.
- *Grid 2* (`C57:G59`), break-even sales by gross margin × rent: `=-($C$25+$C$20*rent_chg)/($B57-$C$15)`. Rent flexes only the rent component of fixed cost. Verified: at −20% rent and 55% GM, (44,450 − 4,400) / 0.494 = 81,073 ✓.
- *Driver ranking* (`C65:F69`) applies a consistent relative ±10% to each driver, which makes the swings genuinely comparable — sales 8,669, gross margin 9,596, rent 4,400, payroll 3,700, variable cost % 926. The conclusion drawn is right; the magnitude quoted in Insight 3 is not (P1-5).

**Cross-check to forecast.** Rows 43–45 convert 13-week forecast store sales to a monthly run-rate of S$83,198 and compare to break-even at −1.9%. A forward-looking check on a historic calculation — good practice, and it agrees with the historic −2.5% closely enough to be credible.

**Hardcodes.** Twelve sensitivity-axis values live locally rather than on Assumptions (P3-4). Everything economically meaningful links centrally.

**Scope note.** Break-even is measured against store direct fixed costs only, excluding shared overhead. That is the correct question for a channel-exit decision and is clearly labelled. ## Formula / Logic Risks

Highest-risk calculation chains, ranked:

1. **`Scenarios!C165:O165` (and S2 equivalent) — store payroll × open factor.** Confuses accrual period with payment period. Materially wrong today (P0-1). Feeds Week 13 cash → Dashboard `A18` → Insight 7.
2. **`13W Cash Flow!C64:O68` — payment timing flags.** Correct for the configured inputs, silently wrong for payment days 26–31, and the guarding control cannot detect it (P2-1, P2-2).
3. **73 `IFERROR(...,0)` wrappers.** Each is a place where a structural break becomes an innocuous zero that the error-scan controls cannot see (P2-3).
4. **`Channel Profitability!F15` vs `13W Cash Flow!C15:O15` — consignment commission base.** Gross in the P&L, net in cash; identical only while the consignment discount input is zero (P2-7).
5. **`INDEX($C$72:$O$72,1,C$5-1)` lag pattern** (used in 6 receipt rows across 4 blocks). Row- and position-anchored; correct now, but an inserted row inside the sales block breaks it with no control coverage.
6. **`MATCH(MIN(range),range,0)` for week-of-lowest-cash** (4 instances). Returns a position that equals a week number only because of the current layout. Also resolves ties to the first occurrence.
7. **`Scenarios!A3` preferred-scenario selector.** A single-metric `MAX` with tie-resolution by position, presented as a determination on a 0.9% difference (P2-11).
8. **`Scenarios!C158:O158` — post-closure inventory.** Replenishment scales by 0.8× but the S$235,000 committed buy does not flex at all. Defensible, undocumented.

## Missing Controls

Recommended additions, in value order:

1. **Receipts completeness** — sales + opening debtors − closing debtors = total receipts, per channel. The highest-value missing cash control (P2-12).
2. **Roll-forward and continuity on Scenario 2 and 3 blocks** — replicate controls 1 and 2 three times. This is the gap that allowed P0-1 (P2-4).
3. **Payroll accrual vs payment** — store payroll paid across 13 weeks equals payroll accrued while the store traded, in every scenario. Directly targets P0-1.
4. **Per-item payment timing** — each monthly item triggers exactly 3×, tested individually rather than in aggregate (P2-2).
5. **Percentage-range assertions** — every rate input between 0 and 1; collection percentages between 0 and 1; payment days between 1 and 28; closure week between 0 and 13.
6. **Error scan on Assumptions** and on the Dashboard chart-data block `A100:C129` (P2-3).
7. **Positive-value assertions on `IFERROR`-wrapped critical paths** — so a masked error cannot present as a clean zero.
8. **Remaining five Dashboard KPI cards** tied to source (P2-5).
9. **Monthly run-rate P&L reconciliation** — `Scenarios!C49` × 6 = `Channel Profitability!G44`. Currently true, untested.
10. **Seasonality index centring** — indices average within a stated tolerance of 1.0, giving the "Avg / check" column an actual check (P3-7).
11. **Sign convention** — all P&L cost lines negative, all cash outflow lines positive.
12. **Scenario 1 neutrality** — all S1 levers at their neutral values (0 or 1), which is the precondition for controls 19–21 being meaningful.

That would take the framework from 30 to roughly 45 controls, but the point is coverage of the untested domains — formula consistency, sign conventions, input ranges and the two scenarios that carry the recommendation — not the count.

## Final Recommendation

**The workbook should not be published in its current state**, and the reason is narrow and specific: Scenario 3 omits S$18,500 of August store payroll, which overstates every exit-case cash figure the Dashboard and Management Insights publish, and none of the thirty controls detects it. Publishing a model under a portfolio banner with a known material error in its headline decision output is the one thing that would do real damage — and it is precisely the error an interviewer probing the exit case would surface.

Beyond that, four commentary statements and one metadata field need attention before release. Two of them (the migration threshold and the driver multiple) are cases where hardcoded prose asserts a magnitude the live model contradicts — the model is right and the words are wrong, which is fortunate, because it means the fix is editorial. The `creator = openpyxl` metadata should be corrected to name a real author, and the generation method should be disclosed honestly in the repository rather than papered over; a parameterised model generator is a stronger portfolio claim than hand-building, not a weaker one.

**To be considered release-ready, the workbook needs:**

1. P0-1 corrected and every downstream figure re-verified.
2. The two new controls (payroll accrual/payment, and roll-forward on Scenarios 2 and 3) so the same class of error cannot recur silently.
3. P1-1 through P1-5 and the chart-count correction resolved.
4. A visual pass in Excel confirming the Dashboard conclusions do not clip and that MODEL STATUS still reads PASS on 32 controls.

That is the complete blocking list. Everything in Should Fix and Optional can follow in a later pass without holding publication.

**Once those are done, this is a strong portfolio artifact.** The cash engine reconciles perfectly across every identity tested. The scenario engine is a genuine rebuild that provably reproduces the base case. The channel P&L makes the right analytical choice on overhead and explains why. The commentary is live-linked, decision-oriented, and in Insight 2 arithmetically self-proving. The input discipline is flawless across 185 cells. The failures found are the failures of a real model — a timing subtlety, some prose that drifted from the numbers, and controls that test what the builder was confident about rather than what they were not. Those are fixable in one focused pass, and fixing them is what will make the finished artifact defensible under exactly the scrutiny it is built to attract.
